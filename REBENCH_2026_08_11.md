**The 1024/batch-4 row is not a usable measurement and its 2.515x should not be
quoted.** Self-spread is 382% on eager and 681% on compiled — round 2 alone read
eager 21124 ms against a 9512 ms median. The run also printed a raw Metal
command-queue description mid-stream, which is what this harness does when a
buffer allocation stalls. At 1024x4 the model is large enough to hit memory
pressure on this machine, and both arms are then measuring the allocator, not
the kernels.

Recorded here rather than dropped, because a 2.5x that nobody can reproduce is
exactly the kind of number that gets repeated later. If this configuration
matters, it needs a machine with more headroom or a smaller `--reps`, run
alone.

**No regressions.** Every row is a win and every row is numerically clean.

The margin shrinks as the problem grows (1.188x -> 1.049x / 1.072x). That is the
expected shape: at batch 1 / 512 the model is launch-and-overhead sensitive,
which is where compilation helps most; at larger footprints both arms converge
on the same bandwidth and conv kernels.

Winning kernels are convolutions in every row — `triton_convolution2d` at 512,
and `triton_conv1x1_nchw` (the DENSE_ROWS=True variant from `5874f2c51d2`) at
1024. Triton takes position 0 of 14 choices in all rows, i.e. it is not winning
by default-only; it beats the alternatives on measured time.

Note the autotune cost: 286-523 s per configuration. Not a runtime concern, but
it dominates wall clock for a sweep and is why this section took most of the
night.

## 3. DistilBert

`distilbert_m4.py`, compiled vs eager, three independent timing methods
(GPU event, wall, per-iter) reported side by side. Spreads are tight (<1% on
eager, <2% on compiled) and the three methods agree within ~1% on every row
except the smallest, so these are trustworthy numbers.

| batch | seq | eager (gpu_event) | compiled | speedup | agreement across methods |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 128 | 53.70 ms | 59.91 ms | **0.896x** | 0.811 – 0.896 |
| 32 | 128 | 214.72 ms | 215.63 ms | 0.996x | 0.996 – 1.064 |
| 8 | 384 | 166.29 ms | 175.10 ms | **0.950x** | 0.950 – 0.955 |
| 32 | 384 | 665.46 ms | 588.60 ms | **1.131x** | 1.131 – 1.132 |

**This is the only model of the three where compiled loses, and it loses on
three of four configurations.**

The shape of it is consistent: compiled wins only at the largest footprint
(batch 32 / seq 384, 1.131x) and is behind everywhere else, worst at the
smallest (batch 8 / seq 128, 0.896x). That is a fixed per-launch cost being
amortised — at b8/s128 the model is small enough that the compiled path's extra
launches and layout moves dominate, and by b32/s384 there is enough arithmetic
to pay for them.

Note the b8/s128 row is also the only one where the three timing methods
disagree materially (0.811 wall vs 0.896 gpu_event). The gap between wall and
device time *is* the launch overhead, and it is 12% of a 54 ms run — consistent
with the reading above.

**Not a regression.** `CLAUDE.local.md` records DistilBert e2e swinging
0.895 / 0.906 / 0.925 / 0.942 / 1.529x across runs on the *old* harness, and
596-603 ms for the b32/s384-equivalent config after the epilogue-fold work
(`965142b`). The 588.6 ms measured tonight is slightly better than that, so
nothing has gone backwards; this model has simply always been the corpus's
weakest row and the older harness was too noisy to show the small-config losses
cleanly.

Worth someone's time later: the b8/s128 launch-overhead deficit is the most
concrete "we are slower" datum in the whole corpus, and unlike the async-DMA
question it has not been chased at all.

## Notes for Fable

### The measurement traps that cost the most time this week

Three separate "results" turned out to be artifacts. All three are now
documented in `CLAUDE.local.md`, but they are worth restating because each one
produced a confident, wrong conclusion that survived several turns:

1. **The env gates are not part of the Triton cache key.** `TRITON_MSL_PIPELINE`,
   `TRITON_MSL_DMA_STAGE`, `TRITON_MSL_UNPIPELINE_A` all change codegen and none
   of them change the cache key. A warm cache serves one arm's kernels to
   another arm. This produced a "the ratio grows with problem size" finding that
   was entirely fabricated. `rm -rf ~/.triton/cache` between arms, always.

2. **Census-gate every DMA claim.** A 294-case sweep was used to compare
   `dma` vs `pipe` arms; the sweep kernel is *masked*, `dmaCopyEligible` rejects
   per-element masks, and the arm emitted **zero** `async_copy_begin_4(` call
   sites. The numbers compared two identical kernels. Grep the emitted MSL for
   the call site *with the paren* before believing any DMA number.

3. **The gates are presence checks.** `TRITON_MSL_DMA_STAGE=0` **enables** it.
   Use `env -u`.

### Async DMA: closed, with a mechanism

The `air.simdgroup_async_copy_2d` staging path is a measured loss on every shape
and the gate stays off. The reason is now specific rather than hand-wavy, and it
is *not* that the DMA is slow:

- The copy is only reachable through the software pipeliner.
- The pipeliner multi-buffers, so B's tile is allocated twice: 16384 B against
  base's 8704 B pool. Against the 32768 B cap that is **2 resident threadgroups
  per core instead of 3** — a 33% occupancy cut paid before any copy issues.
- The entire upside of the copy's overlap is capped at ~2% (an old control that
  deleted the wait measured 0.861 vs 0.876).
- With occupancy *equalised* at BK=16, where the grid supplies only 8 TG/core so
  both arms are grid-limited, base still wins ~5%. So occupancy is not even the
  whole story — the residual is barriers plus per-trip wait latency.

`UnpipelineDotA` (gated, default off) keeps A device-direct while B pipelines
and recovers ~60% of the pipeliner's tax, but `pipeA` (no DMA at all) still
beats `dmaA` on 13 of 20 rows. The async copy does not pay for itself.

### liger CrossEntropy — fixed, and the emitter lesson underneath it

`./run.sh liger` showed CE at **0.61x** while RMSNorm/SwiGLU/RoPE all won
1.3-1.9x. Root cause was a launch constant, not the emitter: liger sets
`MAX_FUSED_SIZE = 65536//2` on any device without its own branch, so
`BLOCK_SIZE = 32768` against a vocab of 32000-128256. That is 32 registers per
thread, past where the reduction stays in registers.

The emitted MSL showed the cliff exactly: `__pool[32768]` pinned at the hard cap
(1 resident TG/core), **2048 scalar threadgroup stores** against only 10
`simd_shuffle_xor`, with a full XOR index expression recomputed per element,
18128 lines. At `BLOCK_SIZE=2048`: zero scalar TG stores, `__pool[8192]`,
1452 lines.

Fixed in liger-kernel (`a80c2d9`), verified on the real harness at
**2.94x / 3.11x / 3.41x**, loss bit-exact.

**The part that is ours** is logged as task #251 — and I had the mechanism wrong
at first. Correcting it here because the wrong version is the more tempting
story.

My initial claim was "the reduce lowering abandons `simd_shuffle_xor` under
register pressure". **That is false.** A plain rowmax over N=32000 at
BLOCK=2048 / 8192 / 32768 emits 5 shuffles, **zero** threadgroup stores, pool
1024, exact at every size. The cross-lane path never degrades.

Censusing the real CE kernel at the two block sizes shows what actually happens:

| | lines | shuffles | pool | TG stores |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BLOCK=2048 | 544 | 10 | 8192 | 18 |
| BLOCK=32768 | 13576 | **10** | 32768 | **1026** |

The shuffle count is *identical*. What explodes is a **`convert_layout` of a
tile too large to stay in registers**: it is spilled through the pool in
8192-element bands, one guarded scalar store per element —

```
if (__f >= 0     && __f < 8192)  v2543[__f - 0]     = v2383;
if (__f >= 8192  && __f < 16384) v2810[__f - 8192]  = ...;
if (__f >= 24576 && __f < 32768) v3086[__f - 24576] = ...;
```

Four distinct threadgroup pointers, a bounds test per element, and the pool
pinned at the 32768 cap (1 resident TG/core).

Three things look reachable: vectorise the banded store (the bounds are
compile-time constants and the run is contiguous), hoist the band predicate out
of the element loop, and ask whether the tile needs banding at all when its
consumer is a reduction that could take it streaming.

Also: `bench_liger_mps.py`'s `peak_memory()` polls `driver_allocated_memory()`,
which is the driver heap quantised to large pages — liger's denominator is
always exactly 16384 (one page) and vanilla either takes a second page ("2.0x")
or trips a 1 GiB heap expansion (1073790976/16384 = **65539.0**, the printed
number). Both ratios are meaningless. With `current_allocated_memory()` the real
saving is 504 MiB vs 26 KiB — genuine and large, just not 65000x.

### The structural defect worth fixing

Four bugs this week were the same bug: **one predicate evaluated twice, at
points where its inputs were not yet equal.** `739091a` (scanPool's panel gate
vs planDot's), `ad518bf` (pool sizing vs pool layout), `cb7173d` (a query asked
while planning vs while emitting), `1eef2f7` (a pad sized with a phase-dependent
input). See `DOT_REFACTOR_PLAN.md` — step 1 addresses exactly this and is worth
doing on its own.

## Addendum: the DistilBert small-batch deficit, resolved

The b8/s128 0.896x row above was flagged as "the most concrete 'we are slower'
datum in the whole corpus". A batch sweep and an aten control settle it, and the
conclusion inverts.

The full curve, gpu_event, Triton-only arm:

| batch | eager | compiled | speedup |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6.98 | 6.00 | **1.163x** |
| 2 | 13.04 | 24.98 | 0.522x |
| 4 | 25.97 | 35.83 | 0.725x |
| 8 | 53.62 | 59.77 | 0.897x |
| 16 | 106.71 | 103.47 | 1.031x |
| 32 | 214.72 | 215.63 | 0.996x |

So it is not a plateau, it is a **trough with its floor at b=2**, recovering
monotonically to parity by b=16. b8's 0.897x reproduces the earlier 0.896x
exactly, so the row is stable, not variance.

The aten-allowed control at the same shapes:

| batch | Triton-only | aten-allowed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6.00 (1.163x) | 6.81 (1.018x) |
| 2 | 24.98 (0.522x) | **125.35 (0.103x)** |
| 4 | 35.83 (0.725x) | **136.18 (0.187x)** |

Letting aten into the choice set is **5.0x slower than our Triton-only arm at
b=2** and **3.8x slower at b=4**. The ~125-136 ms floor barely moving between
b=2 and b=4 is the tell: near-constant cost, not work.

**Verdict: the trough is an inductor-on-MPS small-batch launch floor, not a
Triton kernel deficit, and our path is by a wide margin the better of the two
choices there.** The residual 0.522x/0.725x is what remains after we have
already avoided a far larger aten trap. This row should be read as a Triton win,
not the corpus's weakest result.

Not chased further: what the aten choice actually is at b=2. Worth a look if
anyone cares about sub-8 batch inference, but no corpus model runs there.

## Addendum: task #251 resolved, and the census that closes it

The banded `convert_layout` spill described above is real, and the first of
the three suggested fixes landed as `792960f`: the band-row predicate built
`layoutCoordExpr(rt, r, rowDim)` twice per register per band, and the
coordinate is band-invariant. Naming it once per register cuts the emitted MSL
from 2068544 to 1293240 bytes (**-37.5%**) on a 256x256 fp32 transpose, with an
identical predicate count (4096 either way -- no guard was removed, only the
duplicated expression tree inside each one).

Two corrections to the description above, both worth knowing before anyone
picks this up:

**It is not a reduce-lowering cliff.** Plain rowmax emits 5 shuffles and 0
threadgroup stores at every block size, and sweeping the reduction dimension to
131072 never reaches the path. The trigger is an oversized-tile 2D
`convert_layout` -- a `tl.trans` round-trip reproduces it at 64x64 through
512x512, confirmed each time via `MSL_LOG_REJECT=1`.

**No corpus model reaches it at all.** Counting
`convertLayout / threadgroup-roundtrip` rejects at HEAD:

| model | banded round-trips |
|---|---|
| DistilBert b8 s384 | 0 |
| DiT | 0 |
| BiRefNet | 0 |

So the remaining two fixes -- vectorising the banded scatter via the
`slotStride` test the async-copy path already carries, and the rank-1 mode's
per-register scope -- are **deliberately not done**. They would be the larger
win if the path were hot; it is not hot, it is unreached. The -37.5% is a
compile-time saving on synthetic tiles with no e2e effect on any corpus model.

Reopen only if a real model starts logging that reject.

### A measurement trap worth recording

The first attempt at this re-verification produced numbers that had to be
thrown away, and the tell was in the *control* arm, not ours. Two shapes ran
twice and disagreed by 16% and 25%, and the b8/s384 **eager** baseline moved
160.6 -> 410.7 ms — a 2.6x swing on code neither commit touches. Cause: the run
overlapped a 60-second `ssh` polling loop of my own, at load average 3.07.

Two rules that follow, both already paid for once tonight:

- **Watch the eager arm as the sanity check.** If the baseline moves more than
  a few percent between rounds, the Triton number from that round is noise
  regardless of how plausible it looks. This is the same lesson as the
  `gemm_bench.py` hoisted-`aten_ms` bug (#176), in a different disguise.
- **Run long benchmarks detached (`nohup` + a done-marker), and poll slowly.**
  An `ssh` drop killed the first sweep outright (exit 255) after one shape, and
  the fast poll perturbed the second. Neither is a compiler signal.
