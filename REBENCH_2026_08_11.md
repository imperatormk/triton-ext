## Notes for Fable

### The measurement traps that cost the most time this week

Three separate "results" turned out to be artifacts. All three are now
documented in `CLAUDE.local.md`, but they are worth restating because each one
produced a confident, wrong conclusion that survived several turns:

1. **The env gates are not part of the Triton cache key.** `TRITON_MSL_PIPELINE`,
   `TRITON_MSL_DMA_STAGE`, `TRITON_MSL_UNPIPELINE_A` all change codegen and none
   of them change the cache key. A warm cache serves one arm's kernels to
   another arm. This produced a "the ratio grows with problem size" finding that
   was entirely fabricated. `rm -rf ~/.triton/cache` between arms, always.

2. **Census-gate every DMA claim.** A 294-case sweep was used to compare
   `dma` vs `pipe` arms; the sweep kernel is *masked*, `dmaCopyEligible` rejects
   per-element masks, and the arm emitted **zero** `async_copy_begin_4(` call
   sites. The numbers compared two identical kernels. Grep the emitted MSL for
   the call site *with the paren* before believing any DMA number.

3. **The gates are presence checks.** `TRITON_MSL_DMA_STAGE=0` **enables** it.
   Use `env -u`.

### Async DMA: closed, with a mechanism

The `air.simdgroup_async_copy_2d` staging path is a measured loss on every shape
and the gate stays off. The reason is now specific rather than hand-wavy, and it
is *not* that the DMA is slow:

- The copy is only reachable through the software pipeliner.
- The pipeliner multi-buffers, so B's tile is allocated twice: 16384 B against
  base's 8704 B pool. Against the 32768 B cap that is **2 resident threadgroups
  per core instead of 3** — a 33% occupancy cut paid before any copy issues.
- The entire upside of the copy's overlap is capped at ~2% (an old control that
  deleted the wait measured 0.861 vs 0.876).
- With occupancy *equalised* at BK=16, where the grid supplies only 8 TG/core so
  both arms are grid-limited, base still wins ~5%. So occupancy is not even the
  whole story — the residual is barriers plus per-trip wait latency.

`UnpipelineDotA` (gated, default off) keeps A device-direct while B pipelines
and recovers ~60% of the pipeliner's tax, but `pipeA` (no DMA at all) still
beats `dmaA` on 13 of 20 rows. The async copy does not pay for itself.

### liger CrossEntropy — fixed, and the emitter lesson underneath it

`./run.sh liger` showed CE at **0.61x** while RMSNorm/SwiGLU/RoPE all won
1.3-1.9x. Root cause was a launch constant, not the emitter: liger sets
`MAX_FUSED_SIZE = 65536//2` on any device without its own branch, so
`BLOCK_SIZE = 32768` against a vocab of 32000-128256. That is 32 registers per
thread, past where the reduction stays in registers.

The emitted MSL showed the cliff exactly: `__pool[32768]` pinned at the hard cap
(1 resident TG/core), **2048 scalar threadgroup stores** against only 10
`simd_shuffle_xor`, with a full XOR index expression recomputed per element,
18128 lines. At `BLOCK_SIZE=2048`: zero scalar TG stores, `__pool[8192]`,
1452 lines.

Fixed in liger-kernel (`a80c2d9`), verified on the real harness at
**2.94x / 3.11x / 3.41x**, loss bit-exact.

**The part that is ours** is logged as task #251 — and I had the mechanism wrong
at first. Correcting it here because the wrong version is the more tempting
story.

My initial claim was "the reduce lowering abandons `simd_shuffle_xor` under
register pressure". **That is false.** A plain rowmax over N=32000 at
BLOCK=2048 / 8192 / 32768 emits 5 shuffles, **zero** threadgroup stores, pool
1024, exact at every size. The cross-lane path never degrades.

Censusing the real CE kernel at the two block sizes shows what actually happens:

| | lines | shuffles | pool | TG stores |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BLOCK=2048 | 544 | 10 | 8192 | 18 |
| BLOCK=32768 | 13576 | **10** | 32768 | **1026** |

The shuffle count is *identical*. What explodes is a **`convert_layout` of a
tile too large to stay in registers**: it is spilled through the pool in
8192-element bands, one guarded scalar store per element —

```
if (__f >= 0     && __f < 8192)  v2543[__f - 0]     = v2383;
if (__f >= 8192  && __f < 16384) v2810[__f - 8192]  = ...;
if (__f >= 24576 && __f < 32768) v3086[__f - 24576] = ...;
```

Four distinct threadgroup pointers, a bounds test per element, and the pool
pinned at the 32768 cap (1 resident TG/core).

Three things look reachable: vectorise the banded store (the bounds are
compile-time constants and the run is contiguous), hoist the band predicate out
of the element loop, and ask whether the tile needs banding at all when its
consumer is a reduction that could take it streaming.

Also: `bench_liger_mps.py`'s `peak_memory()` polls `driver_allocated_memory()`,
which is the driver heap quantised to large pages — liger's denominator is
always exactly 16384 (one page) and vanilla either takes a second page ("2.0x")
or trips a 1 GiB heap expansion (1073790976/16384 = **65539.0**, the printed
number). Both ratios are meaningless. With `current_allocated_memory()` the real
saving is 504 MiB vs 26 KiB — genuine and large, just not 65000x.

### The structural defect worth fixing

Four bugs this week were the same bug: **one predicate evaluated twice, at
points where its inputs were not yet equal.** `739091a` (scanPool's panel gate
vs planDot's), `ad518bf` (pool sizing vs pool layout), `cb7173d` (a query asked
while planning vs while emitting), `1eef2f7` (a pad sized with a phase-dependent
input). See `DOT_REFACTOR_PLAN.md` — step 1 addresses exactly this and is worth
doing on its own.
